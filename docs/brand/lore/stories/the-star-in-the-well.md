# The Star in the Well · سهيل في البير

*A tale of the Straw Road. About 1 minute 45 seconds on film.*

At the end of every summer, Suhail rises in the south carrying his waterskin of rain. This year he doesn't. The eight Rafeeqs find out why.

**Look:** classic Disney first (a storybook opening, painted light, warmth, wonder), with Pixar's material richness (felt, suede, brass, sand you can feel) and a DreamWorks-sized comic beat or two.
**Language:** Arabic is the master (narration in a warm storyteller's register; the Rafeeqs speak everyday Saudi Arabic, as they do in the app). The English cut is fully voiced in English.

---

## Prologue: the book

**1. The book opens.** An illuminated star manuscript, gold leaf and lapis, the pages drawn like al-Sufi's *Book of Fixed Stars*. The Straw Road is painted across the spread and begins to glitter.
> **NARRATOR:** يقولون إن في السما قافلة... كل ما مشت، تناثر من أحمالها تبن، وصار درب التبانة.
> *They say a caravan crosses the sky. The straw it spills became the Milky Way: the road the Bedouin call Darb al-Tabbana.*

**2. The Night of Iron.** On the next page the painted sky splits. Sparks fall over the dunes of the Empty Quarter.
> **NARRATOR:** وفي ليلة مطرت فيها السما حديد، طاحت منها شرارات على الرمل.
> *One night, the sky rained iron, and sparks fell to the sand.*

**3. Eight sparks, eight shapes.** Painted vignettes, one after another: a spark in a saddle, a dune, a fanous, a fox's paws, a turn of the wind, a woven blanket, a hood on a tent pole, a wanderer's cloak. Each one opens its eyes.
> **NARRATOR:** وكل شرارة صارت أول شي لمسته... وسمّوهم الرفاق.
> *Each became the first thing it touched. The desert called them Rafeeqs.*

## The adventure

**4. The end of summer.** The painting dissolves into the world. A late-summer night in the dunes, hot and dry. On the highest crest, a small hooded figure faces south: Scout, waiting up.
> **NARRATOR:** وكل آخر صيف، يطلع سهيل من الجنوب شايل قربة المطر... إلا هالسنة.
> *At every summer's end, Suhail rises in the south, carrying his waterskin of rain. But this year...*

**5. Scout sees it.** The southern horizon stays dark. Scout's face falls, then sharpens: far off, something is glowing in the sand.
> **SCOUT:** ما طلع... لحظة، وش ذاك هناك؟
> *He's not coming... wait, what's that over there?*

**6. Across the dunes.** All eight run under the moon: Rider in front, Breeze looping around everyone, Dune half-sliding down every slope, Fennec bounding, Keeper hurrying with his satchel bumping, Lantern's light bobbing, and Drifter at the back, sleep-walking with his eyes shut.
> **RIDER:** هذا بير الهدّار. أعرفه باسمه.
> *That's Bir al-Haddar. I know it by name.*

**7. The well.** An old stone well, alone in the dunes, a faint glow rising from inside it. Fennec's ears swing round.
> **FENNEC:** اسكتوا... أحد يبكي تحت.
> *Shh. Someone's crying down there.*

**8. Down the well.** Looking down the shaft: at the very bottom, a small frightened star, curled up around a waterskin. Keeper is already digging in his satchel.
> **KEEPER:** جبت حبل... احتياط.
> *I brought a rope. Just in case.*

**9. Breeze dives.** Breeze spirals down the rope and wraps the little star in wind. It comes up out of the well in a whirl of light, straight into Lantern's arms.
> **BREEZE:** مسكته!
> *Got him!*
> **LANTERN** *(softly, to the star)***:** لا تخاف. أنا أعرفك.
> *Don't be afraid. I know you.*

**10. Too weak.** The little star flickers. It tries to rise and can't. Everyone looks at everyone. Then, from behind Rider, one eye opens.
> **DRIFTER** *(yawning)***:** طيب... ارموه؟
> *So... throw him?*
> *A beat. Everyone stares at Drifter. Then everyone looks up at the tallest dune.*

**11. The launch.** On the crest of the highest dune. Dune plants himself at the top (*"أطول طعس في الربع! اركبوا!" / "Tallest dune in the Quarter! Everybody on!"*). Rider braces. Breeze spins up a whirlwind. All of them heave together, and the little star is flung up into the night with a great rushing *whoomp*, and the dune booms.

**12. Suhail rises.** The star streaks up over the southern horizon and blooms into the great, bright Suhail. As he settles into his place, his waterskin splits, and clouds come rolling in from the south.

**13. The first rain.** Rain on the dunes for the first time in months. The Rafeeqs dance in it. Fennec is outraged to be wet, then sticks her tongue out to catch a drop.

**14. By the fire.** Later, dry under a tent awning, around a small fire. Suhail shines low in the south. Rain patters.
> **LANTERN:** إذا طلع سهيل...
> *When Suhail rises...*
> **FENNEC** *(dry, wrapped in Keeper's blanket)***:** ...لا تأمن السيل.
> *...don't trust the flood.*
> *Everyone laughs.*

**15. The book closes.**
> **NARRATOR:** الرفيق قبل الطريق.
> *Choose your companion before the road.*

**End card:** Sarjy · سرجي

## Where the story comes from

- **Suhail and his waterskin.** In Arabian folk tradition, al-Jady (the Pole Star) called for help, and Suhail came running with his waterskin; it split, the rain fell, and he had to turn back. His rising over Arabia, late in August, marks the end of the worst heat, and the saying **إذا طلع سهيل، لا تأمن السيل** (*when Suhail rises, don't trust the flood*) warns that the rains are coming. In this story, the Rafeeqs are why he made it.
- **Darb al-Tabbana.** The Arabic name of the Milky Way, from straw spilled along a road by laden animals.
- **The Night of Iron.** The Wabar craters (al-Hadida) in the Empty Quarter, where iron fell from the sky less than 250 years ago.
- **The well with a name.** Rider knows every well by name (his story in the app). Bir al-Haddar, *the roaring well*, is invented for this tale.
- **"Just in case."** Keeper's line in the app's lost-in-the-dunes page.
- **"Wait, what's over there?"** Scout's line in the same page.
- **"When Suhail rises..."** The same saying Umm Saad kept in *End of Winter*.
