## The legend

Of the four that fell on the Night of Iron, one landed somewhere unusual: not in the camp at all, but on a lone traveller walking past it in the dark. The spark fell into the traveller's cloak, the traveller kept walking, and Drifter woke up already on his way somewhere.

He has never really stopped.

Drifter has been everywhere and is in no hurry to be anywhere. He has followed salt caravans and pilgrim roads, ridden in fishing dhows and slept in date baskets, and come back from each one with a story and nothing else. He doesn't collect things. Things are heavy. Stories weigh nothing and get better every time you tell them.

## The long story

Drifter likes to say that he once walked beside a man from Tangier who travelled for nearly thirty years and wrote it all down. Rider has pointed out, more than once, that this was centuries before the Night of Iron.

Drifter says that time runs differently on the Straw Road, that it's a long story, and that he will explain properly after a short nap. He has never explained.

## Why it matters

Drifter looks like he's asleep through every adventure, and mostly he is. But he has taken every wrong turn there is, which means he knows every shortcut. Once in every story, Drifter opens one eye, says the simplest possible thing, and turns out to be right. Then he goes back to sleep before anyone can thank him.

## Who he is

Easygoing and dreamy. When you look at him, he can't be bothered: he just settles a little lower. He loves being stroked and melts into it. When something goes wrong, he shrugs. He naps often, and he hates being rushed.

## Habits

- Yawns, stretches, tilts his head (slowly, every 12 to 20 seconds).
- Breathes slower than anyone.
- His face at rest is **content**.
- His smile is **lazy**: lopsided, eyes half shut.
- First to doze off when the road goes quiet.

## With the others

- **Rider** lets Drifter sleep in his saddle on long walks.
- **Keeper** collects things and Drifter collects stories; between them they are the camp's memory.
- **Scout** finds Drifter deeply confusing. How can anyone not want to know what's over the next dune?
